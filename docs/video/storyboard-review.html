A single note and its direct backlinks are simple. The interesting database question comes when those notes are stored in different places, but users still expect one connected notebook.

Voice-over

For a first version, use a Worker and D1: put note content in a `notes` table and links in a `note_links` table. Save the note and its links together; index the destination for direct backlinks. Or store a notebook’s notes and links in a SQLite-backed Durable Object. That gives the notebook a durable home and a single place to coordinate its updates. Both are sound ways to build the app. While the data and query fit one database, regular SQL is a natural choice.

Voice-over

As the notebook grows, you partition it across several D1 databases or separate Durable Objects. A note in DO A links to one in DO B, which links to another in DO C. A recursive CTE can follow links inside one SQLite database; it cannot join against B’s or C’s private storage. Even a fixed two-hop question now asks the app to route between stores, fetch edges, join results, and keep the read coherent. The challenge is relationships across storage boundaries. A graph query layer can handle that relationship work across the distributed data.

Voice-over

In Poneglyph, a note is an entity, and its details are facts. For `note:heat-17`, the app can assert “is a note,” “has title ‘Heat on my block,’” and “links to `note:canopy-3`.” Each fact carries its source, entity, field, and typed value. The app sends the note and its links through the typed client and gets a committed receipt. Later corrections add evidence rather than silently replacing history. Poneglyph stores the connected data; your app still owns the editor and the note-taking experience.

Voice-over

Now the app asks a two-link question: from “Heat on my block,” follow a link to `Mid`, then follow `Mid` to another note. Poneglyph expresses that as two relationship clauses. It scans for the first link, binds `Mid`, routes that note to its owning shard, and checks the second link there. The matching results join across Durable Objects on one consistent snapshot. Your application asks about the relationship pattern; Poneglyph handles the bounded routing and join.

Voice-over

On Cloudflare, the notes app calls Poneglyph’s Worker API. A graph coordinator routes writes and queries to SQLite-backed shard Durable Objects, and publishes a commit only when the participating shards have applied it. The graph can expand across shards; queries remain bounded and writes pass through one coordinator. Use D1 for conventional note storage in one database. Use a Durable Object when a notebook needs its own coordinated state. Consider Poneglyph when sourced links and cross-shard relationship queries are central to the app. Start with stable note IDs, explicit link facts, and the query patterns your users actually need.
